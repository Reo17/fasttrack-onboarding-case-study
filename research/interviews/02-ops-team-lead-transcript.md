# Interview Transcript: Onboarding Operations Team Lead
Version 1.0 | Status: Final | Author: Debesh Shankhi | Updated: 2026-11-11
Guide: 02-ops-team-lead-guide.md
Duration: ~30 minutes

> **Note**: Harbourline Credit Union is fictional. This interview was conducted as a live role-play: I interviewed an AI playing the Onboarding Operations Team Lead in character, using my interview guide. The stakeholder's answers were not scripted in advance. BA lines are lightly edited for typos only. All findings and analysis are my own.

## Interviewee profile (for consistency, not shared with the interviewee)

- Experience: 11 years at Harbourline, started as a branch teller, team lead for 4 years
- Team: 6 onboarding officers (5.5 FTE)
- Attitude to the project: Sceptic, cautious rather than hostile
- What she cares about most: her team, and not being blamed for delays she doesn't control
- One thing she'll push back on: being told her team is "the bottleneck"

---

## Transcript

**BA:** Thanks for making the time. I'm the business analyst on the FastTrack onboarding project. Today I'd like to understand how your team handles applications now, what makes the work harder than it needs to be, and what matters to your team. You and your team know this process better than anyone, so I'm here to learn from you. This should take about 30 minutes. I'll take notes and send you a summary afterwards so you can correct anything I've got wrong. Before we start, is there anything you'd like to know about the project?

**Ops Lead:** Just one thing. Is this going to be another one where someone tells us we're slow? Because the last review we had, the slide said "onboarding team: bottleneck," and nobody had asked us a single question.

**BA:** That's fair, and it's why I wanted to start with you. I don't have a conclusion yet. I want to understand where the time actually goes, and that includes the parts your team doesn't control.

**Ops Lead:** Okay. Good. Go ahead.

**BA (Q1):** Can you tell me what your team is responsible for when someone applies to become a member?

**Ops Lead:** Pretty much everything between the form and the account. We pick applications up from the inbox, set them up in the tracker, chase documents, check the documents, send them to compliance, and once compliance clears them, we set the account up in core banking and send the welcome email.

There are six of us. Five full-time, one three days a week. One person owns an application from start to finish, mostly. That's deliberate, so the applicant gets one name to deal with. It falls apart when someone's on leave, though.

**BA:** What happens when someone's on leave?

**Ops Lead:** Their applications sit until someone notices. We've tried a shared view, but the tracker's just a spreadsheet, so it's down to whoever remembers to check.

**BA (Q2):** Can you walk me through what happens to an application, from the moment it arrives in the inbox until the account is open?

**Ops Lead:** Sure. The web form comes in as a PDF to the shared inbox. Someone on the team, usually whoever's on inbox duty that morning, opens it, adds a row to the tracker and assigns it.

Then the owner emails the applicant asking for photo ID and proof of address. We have a template. Then we wait. That's the biggest wait, honestly. Some people reply in an hour, some take a week, some never reply at all.

When the documents come in, we check them. Is the ID current, does the name match the form, is the proof of address recent and in their name. If something's wrong, we email them again. Once it's all good, it goes on the daily list to compliance.

Compliance screens in batches, Tuesday and Thursday mornings. So if something's ready on Thursday afternoon, it waits until Tuesday.

**BA:** So an application that's ready Thursday afternoon could wait four or five days for screening?

**Ops Lead:** Yes. And that's not us, but it's us the applicant emails asking where their account is.

When compliance clears it, we key it into core banking. Every field, from the form. About forty of them. Then the account's created overnight in the batch, and the next morning we check it's there and send the welcome email with the account number.

**BA:** And branch applications? Are they the same?

**Ops Lead:** *(laughs)* No. Branches scan paper forms and email them to us. Same process after that, but the scans are sometimes hard to read, so there's more back and forth.

**BA (Q3):** Roughly how much hands-on time does a typical application take your team?

**Ops Lead:** A clean one? Thirty-five, forty minutes all up. Messy ones, well over an hour. I'd say most land somewhere in the middle.

**BA:** Which steps take the most of that time?

**Ops Lead:** Keying into core banking is the biggest single chunk. Fifteen minutes or so, and you have to be careful, because a typo in an address or a date of birth causes problems later. Then checking documents, maybe ten. Setting up the tracker and the first email, five. And then all the chasing. Every follow-up email is a few minutes, and you have to find the thread, re-read where you're up to.

**BA (Q4):** How does information get into the tracker, and when is it updated?

**Ops Lead:** The owner updates it as they go. In theory.

**BA:** And in practice?

**Ops Lead:** In practice, when it's busy, people update it at the end of the day. Or the next morning. So the dates in there are the day someone typed it, not always the day it happened. And if an applicant just goes quiet, nobody closes the row. It just sits there as "awaiting documents" forever. I cleaned out about two hundred of those in June.

**BA:** Is any of that information kept anywhere else?

**Ops Lead:** The emails, obviously. Compliance has their own spreadsheet. And core banking has the account-open date, which is the only date I'd fully trust.

**BA (Q5):** Are there any checks your team does that aren't written down anywhere?

**Ops Lead:** *(pauses)* Yes, a few. Before we key anything in, we search core banking to see if the person's already a member. People forget, or they had a kids' account years ago. If you don't check, you end up with two member records for one person, and that's a nightmare to fix.

And we check the address against the branch areas, so the account gets assigned to the right branch. Branch managers get credit for new members, so they notice if it's wrong.

**BA:** Who taught you those?

**Ops Lead:** Nobody, really. You learn them when you get it wrong the first time.

**BA (Q6):** When an application is sent back for documents, what are the most common reasons?

**Ops Lead:** Proof of address, by a mile. It has to be in their name and less than three months old. A lot of young people don't have a power bill in their name. They're flatting, or living with parents, so they send a bill in someone else's name, or a bank statement that's six months old.

Second is photos of the ID. Blurry, cut off, glare across the face. And then sometimes the name doesn't match. The form says "Josh," the licence says "Joshua Michael." We have to check that's fine, which it usually is.

**BA:** What happens next, and how long does it take?

**Ops Lead:** We email them explaining what's wrong. Then we wait again. Every resend adds days. I'd guess a quarter of applications need at least one resend, maybe more.

**BA (Q7):** Tell me about the last application that was difficult to process.

**Ops Lead:** Last month. A woman who'd recently married. Her licence was in her new name, her bank statement in her old name, and the form had a mix of both. We needed a marriage certificate to link them, which she had to find, scan, and send. Then compliance flagged her old surname as a possible match, which turned out to be nothing. Two and a half weeks, start to finish.

**BA:** How often does something like that happen?

**Ops Lead:** Name changes? A couple a month. Something genuinely messy, maybe one or two a week across the team. They're the ones that take the time.

**BA (Q8):** If parts of the onboarding process changed, what would you want to make sure stays the same?

**Ops Lead:** That applicants can still talk to a person. Especially older ones, or people with complicated situations, like the woman I just mentioned. A system isn't going to sort that out on its own.

And the one-owner thing. When something goes wrong, the applicant should know who to talk to.

*(pause)*

Can I be honest about something?

**BA:** Please.

**Ops Lead:** The team is nervous. They've heard "automation," and they think it means fewer of us. Two of them have asked me directly, and I didn't have an answer.

**BA:** Thank you for telling me. That's an important concern, and I don't want to give you an answer I'm not in a position to give. I'll make sure it reaches the COO, because that's where the decision sits. What I can say is that the messy cases you've described aren't going away, and someone with your team's experience will need to handle them.

**Ops Lead:** That's more than anyone else has said. Just don't let it get lost.

**BA (Q9):** Thinking about past changes here, what helped your team, and what made things harder?

**Ops Lead:** The new email templates last year went well, because one of my team designed them. We tested them for two weeks and changed what didn't work.

The CRM rollout was the opposite. It was decided, configured and switched on. We got a one-hour training session the week before. Half the fields we actually needed weren't there, and we went back to the spreadsheet within a month.

**BA:** What would you want done differently this time?

**Ops Lead:** Involve us early. Show us things before they're finished, and actually change them when we say something doesn't work.

**BA (Q10):** How would you like your team to be involved as the new process is designed and tested?

**Ops Lead:** Let them help map how it really works, not how the procedure document says it works. And let them test it with real cases, the ugly ones. Aroha's been here eight years and knows every weird case there is. She'd be perfect for testing.

**BA (Q11):** A year from now, what would tell you this project had gone well for your team?

**Ops Lead:** Nobody on my team typing forty fields into core banking. Fewer "where's my account?" emails. And my team spending their time on the applications that actually need a person, without being blamed for waits they can't control.

**BA (Q12):** Could I see the tracker and a few example applications after this session?

**Ops Lead:** Yes, I'll give you read access to the tracker. Fair warning, it's messy. I'll pick out a few applications too, a couple of clean ones and a couple of ugly ones, so you see both.

**BA (Q13):** Is there anything I should have asked but didn't?

**Ops Lead:** You didn't ask about the end of the month. Branch campaigns run then, and volume jumps, maybe double. That's when everything backs up. If whatever you build only works on a quiet week, it doesn't work.

**BA (Q14):** Who else should I talk to?

**Ops Lead:** Aroha, from my team, for the detail. And the Riccarton branch manager. Branches see the applicants we never hear from, the ones who give up before they even send the form.

**BA:** Thank you, this has been really helpful. I'll send you the summary by Friday.

**Ops Lead:** Thanks. And thanks for asking us first, for once.
