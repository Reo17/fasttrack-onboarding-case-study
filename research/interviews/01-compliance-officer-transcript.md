# Interview Transcript: AML/CFT Compliance Officer
Version 1.0 | Status: Final | Author: Debesh Shankhi | Updated: 2026-11-10

Duration: ~30 minutes

> **Note:** Harbourline Credit Union is fictional. This interview was conducted as a live role-play: I interviewed an AI playing the Compliance Officer in character, using my interview guide. The stakeholder's answers were not scripted in advance. BA lines are lightly edited for typos only. All findings and analysis are my own.

## Transcript

**CO:** Sorry, monitoring queue ran over. You're the analyst for the onboarding project? I've got until about half past. Go ahead.

**BA:** It's alright. Thanks for making the time. Yes, I'm the business analyst on the FastTrack onboarding project. Today I'd like to understand how AML/CFT screening works for new members now, what makes it hard, and what you'd need to see before any part of it changes. I'll try to wrap everything up within 30 minutes. I'll take notes, and I'll send you a summary afterwards so you can correct anything I've got wrong. Before we start, is there anything you'd like to know about the project?

**CO:** Yes, actually. "Before any part of it changes." So it *is* changing, then. Is this the automated screening idea? Because I heard about that from Member Experience, not from anyone talking to me. And I'd like to know who's going to be signing off on whatever comes out of this. If something goes wrong with an account we've opened, it's my name on the report to the supervisor, not the project's.

**BA:** Yes, it is probably the automated screening idea you've heard of. It is still at an early stage, which is exactly why I would like to learn more about the current compliance monitoring process, so we eliminate such risks in the project. The project will be led by me, owned by Member Experience, and signed off by the COO, if that's what you were asking about.

**CO:** The COO signs it off. Right. So where does compliance sit in that? With respect, the COO isn't the one who answers to the supervisor if we open an account for someone on a sanctions list. If screening rules change, I'd expect that to come through me before anyone signs anything.

And just so we're talking about the same thing: monitoring is a different process. That's ongoing transaction monitoring for existing members. What you're asking about is screening at onboarding. Different team time, different rules. I just don't want the notes mixing them up.

**BA:** Thank you for the clarification. I'll make sure to use the right term, "screening at onboarding", from now on. About the project: the COO is the final sign-off as the sponsor, but we can't proceed without compliance signing it off first. Compliance's role will be a key one, as we want to maintain the same level of screening accuracy, if not better. To do that, we would need compliance to approve the screening rules and the compliance design.

**CO:** Okay. That's better. Approve the screening rules and the design. I'll hold you to that, and I'd like it written down somewhere official, not just in your notes. We've used five minutes on that, which is fine, it needed saying. What do you want to know?

**BA:** It will be in the project charter officially, so it won't just be in the notes. For the first question, could you give a brief introduction to your team: what they're responsible for when a new member applies, how many are on the team, what other duties compete for their time, and how much of the team's capacity screening uses?

**CO:** It's me and two analysts. One's full-time, the other's four days a week. That's the whole AML/CFT function for the credit union, not just onboarding. When someone applies, our job is the screening: sanctions lists, PEPs, adverse media if something looks off. I make the call on anything that isn't clear-cut.

The rest of the week, it's transaction monitoring, suspicious activity reports when we need to file one, staff training, and the annual review of the AML programme. Monitoring is the big one. That can't slip, because the alerts have deadlines.

How much time screening takes, honestly, I couldn't give you a percentage. A fair chunk. It gets fitted in around monitoring, really.

**BA:** That is great to know. Could you walk me through what happens from the moment an application reaches your team to when you clear it? How long does each step usually take, and where are the current wait points?

**CO:** Onboarding sends us an email with a list of applications that have their documents complete: name, date of birth, address, and a link to the documents. We don't screen them the moment they arrive. We do it in batches, a couple of times a week, when the analysts can get off monitoring for a morning.

For each name, an analyst searches the vendor portal. That part's quick, a few minutes a name if there's nothing there. They take a screenshot of the result, save it to the shared drive, mark it cleared in our spreadsheet, and when the batch is done they email onboarding back with the cleared list.

If there's a hit, it comes to me. Those take longer. Could be ten minutes, could be days, depending on what we need. Where does it wait? Mostly before we pick it up, I'd say. And then the hits, if I'm tied up.

**BA:** How do you decide whether a watchlist match is genuine? What information do you look at, and who makes the final call?

**CO:** Most hits are just common names. You'd be amazed how many people share a name with someone on a sanctions list. So the first thing is date of birth. If the list has one and it doesn't match, that's usually the end of it. After that, we look at nationality, any middle names, address history, and sometimes the photo if the list entry has one. For PEPs, it's harder, because it's not just the politician. It's family members and close associates too, so the name on the application might not be the name that matched.

The analysts can clear the obvious ones, like a clear date-of-birth mismatch. Anything uncertain, and every PEP match, comes to me. And if it really is a PEP, that's not the end of it either. That goes up the line, it's not just my call.

**BA:** You mentioned "up the line" for PEPs. Who is up the line, and how can they help with clearing a PEP?

**CO:** Good question, actually. Most people don't ask. If someone's a confirmed PEP, we're required to do enhanced due diligence. That means finding out where their money comes from: source of wealth, source of funds. And we need senior management approval before we open the account. Here, that's the COO, or the CEO if the COO's away. It's in our AML/CFT programme, and it comes from the Act.

So "up the line" doesn't help clear them, it's an approval to take them on. And that's where it really slows down. I need to put a memo together, get it in front of an executive, and wait for a decision. It doesn't happen often, to be fair. A handful a year. But when it does, that applicant can be waiting a couple of weeks.

**BA:** Of the applications you screened last month, roughly how many were real matches? What did the false positive and false negative rates look like? And were there any PEPs in the last month or two?

**CO:** We screened about nine hundred last month, maybe a bit more. Around a hundred and twenty came up with some kind of hit. Genuine ones, two or three. One of those was a PEP, a close relative of a foreign official, and that one's still with the COO now. So the rest of those hundred and twenty were false positives. Mostly common names.

False negatives, though. By definition, I wouldn't know, would I? If the portal didn't flag someone it should have, nobody tells me. That's exactly what worries me about automating any of this. At least now a person looks at every name.

Those numbers are rough, by the way. I'd want to pull the spreadsheet before anyone puts them in a report.

**BA:** What records do you keep for each check, and where are they kept? For example, if you had to find one from three years ago, is there a way to look for it?

**CO:** For every check, there's the screenshot from the portal, saved on the shared drive in a folder for each month. The file name is supposed to be the applicant's surname and the date. Supposed to be. Then there's our spreadsheet, one tab per year, with who screened it, when, and the outcome. For PEPs, the due diligence memo and the approval are in email, mostly.

Three years ago? We could find it. Eventually. If the file was named properly, it's quick. If it wasn't, someone's opening folders one by one. Internal Audit asked for a sample last year, about twenty applications, and it took one of my analysts most of a morning. And we have to keep these for five years, so it's only going to get bigger.

**BA:** That's good to know. Which part of screening takes most of the team's time, and how often does that happen? And could you tell me about a recent application that was difficult to screen: what made it hard, and how was it resolved?

**CO:** The false positives, no question. A hundred-odd a month, and each one's ten, fifteen minutes if it's simple. Longer when the application's missing something we need, like a full date of birth or a previous address. Then we email onboarding, they email the applicant, and we wait. That happens a lot more than it should.

There was one a few weeks ago. A young guy, his name was a close match to a relative of a foreign PEP, a different spelling of the same name. The list entry had no date of birth, so we couldn't rule it out straight away. We needed his place of birth and his parents' names, which we don't normally collect. Onboarding had to go back to him for it. It took about eight days in the end. We cleared him: not the same family at all. But by then he'd emailed to say he'd opened an account somewhere else.

So, it was the right outcome from a compliance point of view. Just not much use to anyone else.

**BA:** Going back to the compliance risks. If some applications were cleared without a manual check, what would you need to see to feel confident in that? What would make you say no?

**CO:** I'll be straight with you. I won't sign off on a black box. If a vendor tells me "trust our algorithm," the answer's no.

What would I need? First, I set the threshold, not Member Experience and not the vendor. Second, for every application cleared automatically, I need to be able to see why: what was searched, what came back, the score, and when. In a form I can hand to an auditor. Third, any hit at all, however weak, comes to a person. Automatic clearing is only for names with nothing on them. Fourth, we review a sample of the automatic clears every month, so we're not just hoping it works. And I want it tested before go-live with names we know should match.

To be fair, for the names with no hit at all, what happens now isn't exactly expert judgment. An analyst types a name into a portal, takes a screenshot and ticks a box. If a system did exactly that, logged it properly, and the hits still came to us, that I could probably live with.

What makes me say no? If I can't see the logic, if someone else controls the threshold, or if there's no proper audit trail. Or if I get the sense it's being rushed through for a deadline.

**BA:** So for you to be confident, you would need to know the logic behind the system. What else would you need to check after the fact to be sure the process was working? How often, and who would do it? And is there anything the law or our AML/CFT programme requires that a new process must not change? Is it written down somewhere I could check later?

**CO:** Not just the logic. The threshold's mine, every hit comes to a person, and there's an audit trail. I'd want all of that in your notes, not just "the logic."

After the fact: the monthly sample, like I said. Say thirty automatic clears a month, picked at random, and one of my analysts re-screens them by hand. If we find one that should have been flagged, we stop and look at why before anything else gets cleared. I'd also want a monthly report: how many applications, how many cleared automatically, how many hits, how many I overrode. That goes in my quarterly report to the risk committee anyway.

And the vendor's lists. Sanctions lists change constantly. I'd need to know how quickly the vendor updates them, and be able to check it.

What can't change? Identity has to be verified before the account opens. Screening has to be finished before the account opens, no "open it now and check later." PEPs still need enhanced due diligence and senior management approval. Records kept for five years. And nothing in the process can tip someone off if we're considering a suspicious activity report. That last one people forget.

It's in our AML/CFT programme document. It's on the intranet, but I'll send you the relevant sections. Easier than you reading eighty pages. We've got about ten minutes.

**BA:** Perfect. So: the logic, the threshold, a manual review on any hit, and an audit trail. Plus a monthly sample to re-check the automatic clears, and a monthly report on the whole process. I'll write that down. I'd love the relevant sections, thank you. A year from now, what would tell you this project went well? What would success look like from your point of view?

**CO:** That's it. Yes. A year from now? No findings from audit or the supervisor about onboarding. That's the first thing, and it's not negotiable.

Then my analysts not spending two mornings a week typing names into a portal. I'd rather they spent that time on the hits properly, and on monitoring, where we're stretched.

And honestly, the hits getting resolved faster. Half the delay on those is us going back for information we could have asked for at the start, like full date of birth, previous addresses, place of birth if the name's a common one. If the application collected the right details the first time, the case from a few weeks ago would have taken a day, not eight.

So: clean audits, my team on the work that actually needs judgment, and nobody waiting a week for something we should have asked on day one.

**BA:** This was great, I have learnt a lot today. If there is anything I should have asked but didn't, please let me know.

**CO:** Two things. You didn't ask what happens when the portal's down. It happens, maybe twice a year, sometimes for a full day. Right now we just wait. If a system's doing this automatically, someone needs to decide what it does then, and the answer had better not be "clear everyone."

And our spreadsheet and onboarding's tracker don't match. Never have. Different dates, sometimes different spellings of names. If you're using their numbers for your baseline, I'd check them against ours first.

You should also talk to the Privacy Officer before you pick any vendor. Where they store people's ID documents is going to be a bigger fight than the screening, I suspect. And Internal Audit, about the audit trail. Better they see it early than at go-live.

Send me the summary by end of the week, and the bit of the charter where it says compliance approves the screening rules. Then we'll see.

**BA:** Thank you very much for your time. I will send you the summary of the interview by end of week, and I'll look out for the relevant sections of the AML/CFT programme when you have time.

**CO:** I'll send those sections tomorrow. And thanks. Honestly, that went better than I expected. You actually listened. The charter bit, though. Don't forget.
