# Interview Guide: Ops Team Lead
Version 1.0 | Status: Completed | Author: Debesh Shankhi | Updated: 2026-11-06
Purpose: understand how the Onboarding Operations team processes applications today, where time and effort go, how the tracker data is captured, and what the team needs from any change.

To know from this interview:
1. How does their team process an application step by step? (needed for As-Is process documentation)
2. Where does the 52 minutes touch time actually go? (OBJ-03)
3. How accurate is the Excel tracker? (A-07)
4. Which messy exception cases come up? (R-05)
5. How does the team feel about the change? (sensitive!) (R-05)
6. Why do applications get bounced for documents? (whats the reason for 27% bounce rate?)

## 1. Opening
Thanks for making the time. I'm the business analyst on the FastTrack onboarding project. Today I'd like to understand how your team handles applications now, what makes the work harder than it needs to be, and what matters to your team. You and your team know this process better than anyone, so I'm here to learn from you. This should take about 30 minutes. I'll take notes and send you a summary afterwards so you can correct anything I've got wrong. Before we start, is there anything you'd like to know about the project?

## 2. Role and context
Q1: Can you tell me what your team is responsible for when someone applies to become a member?
Probe: How many people are on the team? How many of them work on a single application?
Why: context and team capacity.

## 3. Current process
Q2: Can you walk me through what happens to an application, from the moment it arrives in the inbox until the account is open?
Probe: Who does each step? Where do applications wait, and why?
Why: unknown 1, the As-Is process.

Q3: Roughly how much hands-on time does a typical application take your team?
Probe: Which steps take the most of that time?
Why: unknown 2, where the touch time goes (OBJ-03). Compare the answer with the 52-minute baseline.

Q4: How does information get into the tracker, and when is it updated?
Probe: What happens when the team is busy? Is any information kept somewhere else?
Why: unknown 3, how tracker errors could arise (A-07).

Q5: Are there any checks your team does that aren't written down anywhere?
Probe: Who taught you that? What happens if it's missed?
Why: hidden steps and requirements that new staff, or a new system, would miss.

## 4. Pain points
Q6: When an application is sent back for documents, what are the most common reasons?
Probe: Which documents? What does the team do next, and how long does it take?
Why: unknown 5, the 27% bounce rate.

Q7: Tell me about the last application that was difficult to process.
Probe: What made it difficult? How was it resolved? How often does something like that happen?
Why: unknown 4, exception cases the new process must handle (R-05).

## 5. Concerns and constraints
Q8. If parts of the onboarding process changed, what would you want to make sure stays the same?
Probe: Why does that matter to the team?
Why: unknown 6; what the team values, without asking about job security directly.

Q9. Thinking about past changes here, what helped your team, and what made things harder
Probe: What would you want done differently this time?
Why: unknown 6; lessons for the R-05 mitigation and change plan.

Q10. How would you like your team to be involved as the new process is designed and tested?
Probe: Who on the team would be best placed to help?
Why: unknown 6; sets up the R-05 mitigation (team maps the As-Is, leads testing).

## 6. Success and close
Q11. A year from now, what would tell you this project had gone well for your team?
Why: success from the team's point of view.

Q12. Could I see the tracker and a few example applications after this session?
Why: evidence for A-07 validation and the As-Is map.

Q13. Is there anything I should have asked but didn't?

Q14. Who else should I talk to?
